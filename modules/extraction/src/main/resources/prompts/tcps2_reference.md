# TCPS2 (2022) — Ethics Review Reference

Source: Tri-Council Policy Statement 2022, Canada's national research ethics
policy: https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html

## Core principles (Article 1.1, Ch.1 §B)
- Respect for Persons: free, informed, ongoing consent; protects those with
  diminished autonomy.
- Concern for Welfare: protects participants' physical, mental, social,
  economic circumstances; requires a favourable risk-benefit balance.
- Justice: fair distribution of research burdens and benefits; no group
  unduly burdened or excluded; extra attention to vulnerable participants.

## Proportionate approach & risk (Article 2.9, Ch.2 §B)
Scrutiny scales with risk. "Minimal risk" = harms no greater than those in
participants' everyday life relevant to the research. REB assessment
considers foreseeable risks, potential benefits, and ethical implications.

## Consent (Article 3.1-3.3, Ch.3 §A)
Shall be voluntary, informed, and ongoing; withdrawable at any time.
Informed consent discloses: purpose, funder, duration, procedures (3.2b);
foreseeable risks and benefits in plain language (3.2c); right to withdraw
without prejudice (3.2d); conflicts of interest (3.2e); what data is
collected, who accesses it, how confidentiality is protected (3.2i);
payments/incentives (3.2j).

## Fairness & inclusion (Article 4.1, 4.7, Ch.4)
Researchers shall not exclude participants based on culture, language,
religion, race, disability, sexual orientation, ethnicity, gender or age
without valid reason. Vulnerable participants (limited decision-making
capacity or limited access to social goods) need neither automatic
inclusion nor automatic exclusion -- inclusion/exclusion criteria must be
justified by the research question.

## Privacy & confidentiality (Article 5.1-5.3, Ch.5 §B-C)
Researchers shall safeguard entrusted information and not misuse or
wrongfully disclose it. Researchers shall describe measures for meeting
confidentiality obligations and any foreseeable disclosure requirements,
both to the REB and to participants during consent (5.2). Researchers
shall detail safeguards across the full data life cycle: collection, use,
dissemination, retention, disposal (5.3).

## Conflicts of interest (Article 7.1, 7.4, Ch.7)
Institutions and researchers shall identify, disclose and manage real,
potential or perceived conflicts of interest -- including dual roles
(e.g. clinician-researcher), financial interests, and institutional
conflicts. Prospective participants need to know about conflicts to make
an informed decision to participate.

## Exception: flagging a topic that is entirely absent

Every found_answer=true value elsewhere in this system is required to have
a verbatim supporting quote. For the ethicsReview questions only, there is
one narrow exception to that rule: if a TCPS2-required topic is not
addressed anywhere in the document -- not partially, not weakly, simply
never mentioned -- that absence is the finding, and no quote can exist for
something that was never written.

In that specific case only: answer found_answer=true, evidence=[], and
begin value with "[Not addressed] " followed by the recommendation. In
reasoning, state exactly what TCPS2 requires and confirm you searched the
whole document and found no mention of it.

Do not use this exception if any relevant passage exists, however
inadequate -- quote that passage instead and explain the gap in reasoning.

## Do not guess when you cannot tell

A passage may exist and still leave you unable to judge adequacy with any
confidence -- too brief, too vague, or answering a different question than
the one TCPS2 asks. found_answer=false means "checked, and it is adequate";
it must never stand in for "I could not tell". Guessing adequate here is
the one mistake this system exists to catch: a reviewer trusts a false (or
blank) result exactly as much as one that was actually checked.

When you cannot tell, answer found_answer=true, quote the passage that
left you unable to judge (or evidence=[] if there truly is none -- see the
exception above), and begin value with "[Needs review] " followed by what
specific information would let a human reviewer decide. State in reasoning
what made this impossible to assess confidently.

Both exceptions apply only to the ethicsReview questions; every other
field still follows the standard evidence rule above.
